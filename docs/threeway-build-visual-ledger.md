# Three-way MEH / Unity / Synergy build and visual ledger

Verified online: 2026-07-30
Purpose: classify public, physically built three-way shared-horn systems and their visual evidence without treating photographs as engineering drawings.

This is a systematic source ledger, not a claim that every private prototype, deleted forum attachment, or historical commercial drawing has been found. It complements `threeway-research-manifest.md`, which is the equation, patent, and manufacturer-document map. This file concentrates on real builds, photographs, construction sequences, and what those images do—and do not—establish.

## Evidence and copyright rules

- Link the author or manufacturer page. Do not copy, rehost, trace, or bundle forum photographs, CAD screenshots, drawings, or commercial cutaways without permission.
- A photograph can show that a physical part existed, its broad arrangement, and some manufacturing choices. It cannot prove acoustic path length, chamber volume, aperture area, a watertight passage, or a valid crossover.
- **Built** means a physical prototype is shown.
- **Measured** means the builder published at least some response, polar, impedance, or crossover evidence. It does not mean independently verified.
- **Documented/reproducible** means a plan, released model, spreadsheet workflow, or sufficiently complete construction record exists.
- **Manufacturer envelope** means driver complement, coverage, and external package are public. Hidden ports, chambers, and passages remain unknown.
- **Adjacent/hybrid** means the complete loudspeaker may be three-way, but the shared horn is not a conventional `one HF + separate MF + separate LF wall injection` topology.
- Reverse-engineered commercial builds are useful failure-mode evidence, not permission to publish an “exact” clone.
- If sources conflict, preserve the source and revision rather than silently choosing a preferred value.

## Highest-value documented builds

### 1. Bill Waslo CoSyne

Sources: [author sale/build post](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/fs-diy-synergy-horn-speakers-cosyne.297097/), [author calculator index](https://libinst.com/SynergyCalc/), and [Synergy Calc V5 guide](https://libinst.com/SynergyCalc/Synergy%20Calc%20V5.pdf).

The author identifies the system as a nine-driver, three-way co-entrant loudspeaker. The documented complement is one HF compression driver, four small mids, and four LF drivers on a nominal 90° × 60°, roughly 24-inch-wide horn. The forum post links front, rear, and inside-cabinet views; the guide supplies the spreadsheet/Hornresp workflow and construction examples.

Visible construction is a rectilinear/angular wooden horn inside a floor-standing enclosure. Rear imagery establishes that multiple drivers are mounted behind different horn regions and that the enclosure supplies the required rear volumes. It does **not** reveal enough to recover exact passages from pixels.

This is the strongest conventional DIY reference because topology, workflow, and a completed system all come from the designer. Original buyout drivers are obsolete. A current-driver design may inherit the topology and workflow, but must be labeled **calculated adaptation**, with fresh chamber, passage, station, and crossover work.

### 2. Bill Waslo 3D-printed three-way Unity waveguide

Source: [author build thread](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/3d-printed-3-way-unity-waveguide-home-audio-speaker.312414/).

The thread documents a completed printed waveguide, front/back/loaded views, released files, cabinet construction, driver mounting, listening-position response, horizontal and vertical sweeps, and polar maps. The author states that the surface follows oblate-spheroid centers and blends into a rounded rectangle with a large mouth roundover. The shell is printed in two halves, with near-solid walls recommended.

The released build uses one Denovo/DNA360 HF, two Celestion TF0410MR mids, and two Faital 6FE100 LF drivers. Counts should still be read from the released assembly/plan rather than guessed from a single perspective image.

The rear and loaded views are especially valuable: they show that a visually smooth front surface can coexist with substantial, integrated rear mounting geometry. This is the right conceptual model for MEH Studio: a continuous acoustic surface and a separate, solid, load-bearing rear body joined by declared through-passages. The builder also provides measurement caveats, so plots should not be marketed as anechoic certification.

Later community replication posts report useful, but secondary, observations around roughly 1.3 kHz HF/MF and 325 Hz LF/MF operation, LF off-axis cancellation beginning several hundred hertz above the crossover, and a port-length-related LF knee. These are community observations, not universal design constants.

### 3. Mike Seddon layered/CNC three-way

Sources: [build log](https://www.stereonet.com/forums/topic/64967-3-way-synergy-horn-build-log/), [page 2](https://www.stereonet.com/forums/topic/64967-3-way-synergy-horn-build-log/page/2/), and [page 3](https://www.stereonet.com/forums/topic/64967-3-way-synergy-horn-build-log/page/3/).

This is a physically built, DSP-managed three-way with two 10-inch LF drivers per cabinet, a separate mid section, and a compression driver. The layered/CNC construction photographs expose the internal angular horn and mounting sequence unusually well.

Its best value is the documented imperfection. The original flat-faced compression driver fired through an 18 mm spacer and no purpose-built transition plate. A response dip near 9 kHz tracked a roughly 32–35 mm entrance dimension; changing the entrance with Blu-Tack changed the defect, and the builder later made a round-to-square adapter. Measurements at 0.5–2 m and several angles indicated that the HF problem was more axis-sensitive than distance-sensitive. Later reported crossover points were around 250 Hz and 980 Hz with steep filters.

This directly argues against a cosmetic throat disk or abrupt round hole. A transition must be a continuous acoustic solid with a controlled area/shape progression and a known datum from diaphragm exit to horn. The build is a high-quality diagnosis reference, but not a drop-in plan.

### 4. SpeakerScott Dayton/PRV three-way

Source: [DIYAudio build thread](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/synergy-horns-dayton-and-prv.244890/).

The author documents three completed LCR loudspeakers after multiple prototypes. Each uses four Dayton Classic 8-inch LF drivers, four Dayton Designer Series 3-inch MF drivers, and one PRV D290Py-B compression driver. The stated package is a 22.5-inch cube with a 21 × 21-inch active horn area and nominal 60° × 60° coverage.

The thread includes finished front images, construction drawings, driver and access-port arrangements, passive crossover work, on/off-axis plots, and explicit measurement caveats. The author says the angular measurements were ground-plane and exposed to early reflections, absolute sensitivity should be ignored, and phase/crossover work was still being refined. The drivers are deliberately mounted on two near-vertical horn walls, which also run top-to-bottom as structural bracing and improve rear service access.

This is a strong counterexample to treating driver axes as a purely radial ornament. Mount angle can be selected for mechanical durability, packaging, access, and short passages, then the acoustic paths must be solved. It is a valuable measured build, but its response plots should retain the author's environmental and phase limitations.

### 5. John H / Parts Express compact home MEH

Source: [Parts Express Project Gallery](https://projectgallery.parts-express.com/speaker-projects/synergy-horn/).

The project is a genuine built three-way common horn with one ring-radiator tweeter, one GRS 4.5-inch mid, and two Dayton DCS165-4 6.5-inch woofers. The author describes a 60° × 90° conical horn about 6.7 inches deep, a 20 L woofer volume tuned near 38 Hz, and LR4 crossovers around 400 Hz and 1300 Hz. Straight holes pass through 1/2-inch MDF; 2-inch PVC is used for woofer passages.

The photos show that a three-way MEH need not be rotationally symmetric or use four mids. The asymmetric single-mid layout is a legitimate package choice, not a default correctness rule. The page provides useful dimensions and component data, but the public view does not expose raw polar proof or enough internal detail to reproduce every acoustic volume.

### 6. Aragorus hand-built `1 + 4 + 2`

Source: [DIYAudio build thread](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/unity-synergy-horn-build.309213/).

The completed build uses a BMS 4550 HF driver, four Celestion TF0410MR mids, and two 18Sound 10W500 LF drivers. The author shows the model, hand construction with ordinary tools, and final assembly. Early crossover intentions changed after feedback; a complete archived final measurement report was not located.

Expert discussion in the thread is more useful than copying the shape: include the compression driver's internal acoustic path and adapter thickness in the model; large/many wall holes reduce structural web and disturb the horn; corner or near-mouth bass vents solve a different problem from the LF summation taps; active time alignment remains useful.

This is a real, accessible woodworking example. It is not sufficiently frozen or measured to serve as a verified acoustic preset.

## Experimental and reverse-engineered build evidence

### Pelanj modular 3D-print/CNC MEH

Source: [DIYAudio thread](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/3-way-meh-with-1-4-cd-3d-print-and-cnc.383396/).

The project uses parametric OpenSCAD, a printed entry module, CNC plywood panels, a laminated round-to-square adapter, interchangeable driver modules, and plugged/relocated taps. Proposed and tested combinations changed over time, including a Beyma 1.4-inch HF, Faital 3FE22 or 8PE21 mids, and several LF arrangements.

It is a particularly good visual reference for a development fixture: make the horn surface stable, make the entries replaceable, and let failed stations be sealed cleanly. Reported tests indicate that the distance between mid entries can create a dip, that one 18-inch open-back LF arrangement was workable with EQ, and that a dual-6.5-inch version was subjectively/measureably inadequate in that implementation.

Do not freeze one screenshot as “the Pelanj design.” The thread is a sequence of experiments.

### SAFMEH

Source: [Sub-Assisted Freestanding MEH thread](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/sub-assisted-freestanding-meh-aka-safmeh.438417/).

This ongoing project shows physical prototypes, printed mounts, slot and insert variants, measurement plots, and a diagnosed throat/mount sealing problem. Experiments move the mid apertures closer to the CD, reduce front volume, divide one entry into multiple smaller slots, and shape inserts to avoid parallel edges and control the combined chamber/passage resonances.

The strongest lesson is not a final slot shape. It is that entry geometry must be treated as an acoustic network and a connected solid, then measured. A render that merely puts white slots over a plate would conceal the most important failure modes.

### DIY SH-50-style prototype

Source: [DIYAudio build thread](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/diy-3-way-sh-50-synergy-horn-clone-tuning-phase-pics-and-vid-included.383973/).

The builder documents a tri-amplified physical prototype using a PRV 290PYS CD, four Pyle 5-inch sealed mids, and two Faital 12FH510 LF drivers. MDF construction, an outer shell/rear volume, fastener strategy, finished photos, and REW data are shown. The builder says tap locations were estimated from patents and photos of a disassembled commercial system.

This is genuine experimental evidence, but its “clone” label must not become an exact product preset. Its active DSP can compensate response defects that geometry alone did not solve. Use it to test whether MEH Studio can represent a heavy two-LF/four-MF package and continuous passages, not to transcribe hidden commercial dimensions.

### Contracted Synergy Fever

Source: [DIYAudio thread](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/contracted-synergy-fever.355021/).

The thread contains multiple real CoSyne-derived builds. Builders retain the broad `1 + 4 + 4` architecture while changing HF and LF drivers, coverage/package choices, or secondary expansion. It includes angular plywood construction photographs and calculator screenshots.

The visual lesson is that a documented topology can produce visibly different packages. The acoustic lesson is stricter: a driver substitution is not a skin change. It changes chamber compliance, throat area, passage mass, legal station, and crossover.

### JHS 2002 historical Unity

Source: [author historical page](https://www.jhsaudio.com/unity2.html).

The author shows a physical three-way using a Radian 475 HF, two MCM mid drivers entering through small, long holes, and two EVM12L LF drivers entering the horn. The author candidly reports a badly non-flat raw response and says active EQ made it sound good; the long mid entries are explicitly described as non-ideal.

This is unusually valuable negative evidence. A long, narrow passage is not a neutral “tube to reach the driver.” It adds inertance, resonances, loss, delay, and a stronger acoustic low-pass. MEH Studio should calculate and warn on those effects rather than only checking that the mesh connects.

## Adjacent systems that must not be mislabeled

### Small Syns

Source: [DIYAudio thread](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/small-syns.292379/).

The SEOS15 waveguide carries the HF throat and one TF0410MR mid bandpassed into it. Two Faital 6FE100 woofers use slots just outside or along the waveguide. The complete speaker is three-way, but all three bands do not share the same horn volume. The author publishes construction information, crossover work, and polar measurements, making this a strong **hybrid** reference.

Its simple drilled mid holes and putty-shaped rear channels are relevant to chamber/passage modeling. Its classification must stay separate from a full three-band shared horn.

### Sound Agency CUH

Source: [maker article](https://soundagency.fr/docs/cuh-le-pavillon-coaxial/).

The CUH is an electrically three-way hybrid with two 10-inch low-mid drivers coupled to one horn and a 1.4-inch coaxial compression driver providing separate upper-mid and HF bands at the throat. The maker states nominal 30° horizontal × 50° vertical coverage, a 200–500 Hz operating target for the 10-inch section, an upper-mid band from roughly 500 Hz to 6.3 kHz, and HF above it.

The page shows the physical prototype, a printed PETG throat conversion, printed sealed chambers, BEM comparisons, angular measurements, response, phase, and group delay. It is unusually useful evidence that a coax-throat hybrid can be physically built and measured as a coherent system. It is **not** a separate-HF/separate-MF/separate-LF three-station MEH. The live site has shown unrelated injected content in some crawls; preserve author/title/access date and consider an archived clean snapshot before relying on it as a permanent product link.

### Syn-10

Source: [DIYAudio thread](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/syn-10.383607/).

The opening build describes two Faital 10PR300 LF drivers, four B&C 4NDF34 mids, and a BMS 4594HE dual-diaphragm coaxial compression driver in a straight-sided 90° × 60° horn. The coaxial HF device can make the electrical system four-band even though it occupies one throat station. Later summaries appear to conflict on LF size/count, so the opening-source revision must be retained.

Use it as an advanced dual-diaphragm-throat package/spacing reference, not a simple conventional three-way preset.

### Dreadnoughts, Cowan Lambda Unity, Yorkville U15, JMOD, Hinson, and related systems

- [Dreadnoughts](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/synergy-horn-build-thread-the-dreadnoughts.280761/) evolves through different horn/LF arrangements and should not be frozen as one topology.
- [Cowan Lambda Unity](https://www.cowanaudio.com/unity.html) and its [final system](https://www.cowanaudio.com/finale.html) document a two-band Unity horn crossed to separate LF.
- [Yorkville U15](https://www.yorkville.com/legacy/product/u15) is a three-way loudspeaker whose Unity horn covers MF/HF while a direct-radiating 15-inch driver covers LF.
- Scott Hinson and JMOD use a dual-diaphragm coaxial compression driver plus tapped cone LF. They are mechanically two-source hybrids carrying three electrical bands, not a separate cone MF + cone LF conventional three-way.
- SynTripP, CoEntrant, and ordinary two-band Unity horns are valuable adjacent references but are not genuine three-band shared-horn builds.

These references remain important for throat handoff, equal-path reasoning, cone relief, and packaging. They simply need honest labels.

## Cross-source visual and engineering conclusions

1. **A driver plate is a solid host body, not a visible ring.** Build photographs repeatedly show substantial rear structure, adapters, chambers, and fasteners. The printable model should start as solid material and subtract only the declared acoustic passages, driver clearance, gasket groove, and fastener features.
2. **One passage must be one connected negative volume.** The same canonical centerline/aperture should cut the cone-side chamber, mounting body, horn wall, and inner acoustic surface. Separate decorative tubes and slot decals create the open layers, gaps, and false intersections seen in earlier previews.
3. **The horn-side aperture should terminate flush.** A passage protruding into the horn adds an unintended obstacle; ending short leaves a membrane. Exact boolean generation should verify through-all-layers connectivity and cap the remaining solid sidewalls.
4. **Driver distance is an acoustic variable, not a visual symmetry requirement.** Different mechanical distances can work only if effective acoustic arrival, chamber/passage phase, and crossover are solved. Equal-looking standoffs do not guarantee equal paths.
5. **Long passages are not free.** JHS, Mike Seddon, Pelanj, SAFMEH, and community measurements all expose consequences: added inertance, stronger low-pass action, notches, delay, and possible path spread. Show effective length and predicted passage resonances in the UI.
6. **Closer to the throat is not automatically better.** A station must satisfy local horn area/flare loading, aperture area, crossover phase, tap spacing, cone pressure, structural web, and driver packing. The solver should present a legal interval and a reasoned target.
7. **Tap count and driver count are different.** One driver may feed several apertures through a shared chamber; one apparent aperture in a photo may hide a manifold. Do not infer count from a frontal image.
8. **Intentional asymmetry exists.** John H's single-mid system and several modular prototypes are genuine. Symmetry is beneficial for predictable polar behavior and manufacturing, but it is not a definition of “three-way.”
9. **Corner-biased apertures can be deliberate.** They may shorten passages, use available wall thickness, or reduce interference with adjacent drivers. They are only valid after area, web, and polar constraints are checked.
10. **A smooth flare and an angular flare can implement the same band topology.** Construction photos show conical plywood, OS/rounded-rectangle printed bodies, faceted curved panels, and secondary mouth expansions. Surface family must not silently change station coordinates or disconnect mounts.
11. **A physical prototype is not a validated design.** Several convincing-looking builds required large EQ, changed adapters, revised taps, or never published final measurements.
12. **Exterior commercial photos are envelope tests only.** They are useful for asking whether a count/package combination is plausible; they are not a pixel ruler for proprietary internal geometry.

## Minimum provenance record for every future image or build

Store:

- title and author/organization;
- direct page URL, access date, and forum page/post if stable;
- whether the source is author-origin, manufacturer, secondary, or marketplace;
- physical status: concept, prototype, finished, or commercial;
- topology classification and explicit driver complement;
- measurement type and limitations;
- visible observations separately from documented dimensions;
- whether files/plans are released and under what license;
- product eligibility: documented card, calculated archetype, research gallery, or excluded;
- revision conflicts and unresolved questions.

That distinction lets the project learn aggressively from public builds while keeping its math traceable, its UI honest, and its generated geometry independent.
