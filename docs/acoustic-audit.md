## RMS, interfaces and motor loading

All phasors use `exp(+jωt)` and RMS amplitudes. Pressure is Pa, volume velocity Q is m³/s, acoustic impedance is Pa·s/m³, electrical impedance is Ω. Average power is `Re(p conj(Q))` or `Re(V conj(I))`, with **no 1/2**. Peak displacement is `sqrt(2)|Q|/(ω Sd)` and peak section velocity is `sqrt(2)|Q|/S`. SPL is `20 log10(|p_RMS| / 20 µPa)`.

Both local FEM flows point into its air domain. Cone flow points toward the horn, and local outlet flow is **minus** the flow injected into the horn. The coupled equations and their real-power audit consistently follow these signs. Negative real power at one actively driven branch is possible through mutual coupling; a passive complete system must still balance total power. A muted branch must retain its motor impedance and acoustic load.

The insert mesh projected cone area is **0.01319469958 m²**, compared with catalog Sd **0.0132 m²**, a ratio of **0.99959845**. Its curved cone surface area is **0.01380885136 m²** and is not a piston-volume area. The existing transformation `diag(Sm/Sd,1) Z diag(Sm/Sd,1)` is correct: it preserves reciprocal matrix symmetry, force and power. The open collector has its own projected area **0.01317880599 m²**, requiring its own transform. A geometric surface area substitution would be incorrect.

The outlet FEM uses a uniform **normal** velocity basis over a nonplanar fan of area **0.00165567575 m²**. Its projected section is **0.00164735075 m²**, 0.505% smaller. `Q/actual area` is the mean normal interface velocity; `Q/projected section` is a section-speed proxy. Neither is a peak gap velocity. The 2.75 mm cone gap needs a spatial velocity solution to find local maxima.

The manufacturer's 8 Ω data are consistent with the catalog: Fs 72 Hz, Re 5.2 Ω, Qms 11.5, Qes 0.44, Mms 17 g, Bl 9.5 Tm, Le 0.6 mH, Sd 132 cm², Vas 7 L. The engine obtains Cms=0.287426 mm/N and Rms=0.668749 Ns/m from Fs/Mms/Qms. They imply Vas=7.09396 L and Qes=0.443116; these small differences follow catalog rounding. Preserve one internally consistent parameter set instead of independently imposing all rounded values. Source: [B&C 6NDL38 8 Ω data sheet](https://bcspeakers.com/en/products/lf-driver/6-5/8/6ndl38-8.pdf).

Mms includes the air loading of the parameter measurement. A FEM air domain should couple to **dry mechanical mass Mmd**, or use an explicitly declared effective-mass convention. [COMSOL's lumped-driver formulation](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.models.aco.lumped_loudspeaker_driver/lumped_loudspeaker_driver.html) distinguishes these quantities and couples diaphragm force and velocity to the air field. [Klippel's calibration guidance](https://docs.klippel.de/db-lab/latest/transducer-parameter-identification/fastlsi/fastlsi.html) likewise warns that acoustic surroundings change identified moving mass.

For an illustrative equivalent circular piston of radius 64.82045 mm, the low-ka infinite-baffle added mass is `8ρa³/3` = **0.874443 g per face** at ρ=1.204 kg/m³. Two independent faces would give **1.748885 g**, 10.288% of 17 g, and assumed Mmd **15.251115 g**. This is a *sensitivity assumption*, not an authenticated B&C correction: actual free-air mounting, basket and cone affect the result. Recompute the system with 0/one-face/two-face correction as a sensitivity range, retain Cms/Rms inferred from the original catalog values, and expose an override for measured Mmd. Recomputing Cms to retain 72 Hz after removing air mass would silently alter the suspension. A rear compliance alone omits distributed rear inertance, so subtracting both faces without resolving rear air can also undercount loading.

## Horn and exterior form one load

The saved nominal mouth diameter is 700 mm. The axial solver truncates the returning lip at the profile's largest axial coordinate, **z=358.70424 mm, radius=304.83731 mm**. The final lip is at z=296.10730 mm/radius=350 mm and is excluded. Thus its actual reduced radiation boundary is **609.67461 mm diameter**. Any far-field projection must use that same boundary and the solved mouth flow; using a 700 mm radiator with this load would be inconsistent.

The existing baffled load is the uniform-piston radiation impedance into an infinite rigid baffle. Its compatible far-field pressure is `p = j ρ ω Qmouth exp(-jkr)/(2πr) × 2J1(ka sinθ)/(ka sinθ)`. It predicts an **assumed piston** observer response, not the real horn's directivity. The RMS far-field hemisphere power integral equals `Re(Zradiation)|Qmouth|²`; `tests/physics-audit.cjs` checks this independently with a Bessel-series angular integral. The [primary baffled-piston verification example](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.models.aco.baffled_piston_radiation/baffled_piston_radiation.html) documents the matching pressure, impedance and radiated-power formulation. A matched absorbing load is useful numerically but does not supply a physical far-field radiator.

Do not separately radiate four side ports into free space and then add a disconnected horn response. They drive the *same* interior field, and the exterior load must feed back into their mutual acoustic impedance. Closed throat is an assumed passive boundary, not the true idle BMS4594HE source impedance. Its calibrated complex acoustic source and crossover are unavailable. The state selecting an HF driver is insufficient to synthesize HF SPL or crossover summation. The sealed rear has no separate rear radiation; a later vented configuration requires coherent vent/mouth radiation and their exterior mutual loading.

Editor total front air is **199.231898 cm³**, while its lumped cavity compliance excludes the neck and uses **181.111039 cm³** plus **18.120858 cm³** neck volume. The differences are explained by curved root closure and polygonal cone, not an unexplained correction factor. A native matrix already includes its entire front passage. Never add a second cavity, neck inertance or empirical end correction over it. Its reduced horn-side interface still omits the spatial junction and may need a justified junction correction in a surrogate.

The inherited matrices at the start of this branch are solved and qualified only at **100, 300, 500, 700, 1000 Hz**. Coarse/medium/fine DOFs are 22,695 / 47,628 / 98,721. The final matrix changes are respectively **0.001011%, 0.009382%, 0.027637%, 0.058768%, 0.137252%**, and reference-loaded phase changes are **0.000202°, 0.004411°, 0.014758°, 0.028671°, 0.052075°**. These are refinement differences, not absolute physical error bounds. The fine mesh has very small minimum SICN 0.000279 despite mean 0.74957; matrix convergence does not certify local gradient/peak-velocity accuracy. The lowest recorded closed-domain modes are about 2049/2050 Hz. Their distance from the five sample frequencies does not prove that the *loaded* system has no inter-sample resonances. New denser results, if delivered separately, supersede this inherited sampling inventory rather than retrospectively qualifying it.

Exact samples must remain visually distinct from any fitted or interpolated reduced-model curve. A broadband passive surrogate can be useful for tuning if its formulation, fitting frequencies and pointwise residuals are disclosed. A five-point match cannot validate its inter-sample behavior or geometric extrapolation. Geometry sweeps using such a model are screening candidates for new solves, not new converged FEM designs.

## Practical route to higher fidelity

1. Keep the coupled motor/front/shared-rear/axial horn model for fast parameter sweeps. Include positive loss assumptions, feasible geometry, source phase/delay, explicit port-area and length conventions, target-band ripple and minimum output, and per-branch excursion/velocity headroom. Separate scalar objective weights from hard constraints. Port velocity limits are editable engineering thresholds; a linear model does not predict turbulence or compression.
2. Use 2D axisymmetry for genuinely rotationally symmetric calibration cases and the axisymmetric horn's modal study. It cannot replace discrete four-side entries. Equal coherent fourfold sources can excite azimuthal orders 0, ±4, ±8…; independent phases or imperfect symmetry also excite lower orders. An m=0 ring source misses those fields. Azimuthal Fourier methods can represent multiple orders only when geometry/material symmetry supports their separation. [The governing axisymmetric equations](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.aco/aco_ug_pressure.05.164.html) explicitly retain a selected azimuthal order.
3. The recommended next spatial model is a 3D interior containing all four front passages, actual horn junctions and full rolled lip, coupled to one exterior BEM radiation operator or a sufficiently large exterior FEM region with a tested PML. Drive one cone at a time to extract a reciprocal four-source impedance matrix plus observer transfer vectors; include the throat as an additional calibrated/passive port when data exist. Couple this matrix to all motors and the shared rear in one solve. Interior/exterior pressure and normal velocity continuity must be enforced at their common interface. This is an engineering recommendation from the physical coupling requirements, not a delivered full-horn solver. [FEM–BEM formulation research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03523) describes such a coupled Helmholtz strategy.
4. Resolve or model viscothermal loss in the narrow gap. Lossless rigid-wall pressure FEM cannot establish it. Boundary-layer impedance/narrow-region reductions can be practical where applicable; selected 3D thermoviscous reference solves should verify them. [COMSOL's coupling guidance](https://www.comsol.com/blogs/modeling-speaker-drivers-which-coupling-feature-to-use) discusses the narrow-channel requirement.
5. Converge interior mesh, interface modes, exterior/PML discretization or BEM resolution, and frequency sampling separately. Check reciprocity, passivity, power, source-area normalization and off-axis complex response, with dense/adaptive frequencies around impedance/phase features. Quarter-sector symmetry is allowed only for matching geometry *and excitation*; arbitrary multi-source phases require full domain or correctly phased periodic sectors.

The zero-addition comparison holds the two model interface conventions equal and is the appropriate default for this comparison. Even the zero-addition case needs denser frequencies before claiming a bandwidth/error envelope. The common simplified horn/exterior can cause common errors invisible in this comparison.

Report the voltage ceiling as **in-band linear headroom**: current constraints are evaluated within the selected band, and no protective high-pass/low-pass crossover exists. Outside-band excursion must not be ignored when applying a broadband signal. A 17 m/s *peak section-average* speed threshold is an editable screening target, not a turbulence or damage guarantee. A custom per-source axial-position study is network sensitivity unless the resulting mixed array receives a joint geometry/collision check; independent per-station symmetric-array checks are insufficient. Do not label entry speed as maximum internal passage or gap speed.
